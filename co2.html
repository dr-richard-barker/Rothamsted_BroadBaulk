Why This Matters

Over Broadbalk's 180+ year lifespan, atmospheric CO₂ has surged by ~50%. Because wheat is a C₃ plant, elevated CO₂ directly stimulates RuBisCO efficiency and suppresses photorespiration, theoretically boosting yields.

However, Broadbalk's long-term dataset provides a crucial empirical reality check. The unfertilized Nil plot (Plot 3) has remained completely flat at ~1.0-1.4 t/ha for 180 years, demonstrating that without adequate nitrogen, plants cannot utilize the extra carbon. Recent studies by Addy et al. (2021) using the Sirius wheat model isolated the CO₂ effect from weather, showing a modest ~9.4% contribution.

This aligns with Free-Air CO₂ Enrichment (FACE) experiments, which show that real-world yield stimulation is often ~50% lower than enclosed chamber experiments previously predicted.

Attributing the CO₂ Effect

Sirius wheat modeling (Addy et al., 2021) allows us to "turn off" the historical CO₂ rise to see its true impact. By holding CO₂ constant at 1892 levels (295 ppm) but using historical weather, the model isolates the specific contribution of CO₂ fertilization.

The gap between the scenarios reveals that CO₂ contributed approximately 9.4% to simulated yield gains over the long term. This is significant, but a small fraction of the ~300% total yield increase driven by genetics and agronomy.

Key Insights

CO₂ Fertilization is Nitrogen-Limited

Low-N plots see almost zero benefit from elevated CO₂.

FACE vs. Chamber Mismatch

Open-field CO₂ stimulation is ~50% lower than chamber experiments.

The Real Growth Engine

Semi-dwarf genetics + synthetic N drove the 300% yield revolution.
